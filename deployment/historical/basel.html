Constitutional Foundation

The Plebeian Tribunal operates within the framework of the South African Constitution, specifically recognizing the supremacy of the Constitution and the Bill of Rights enshrined in Chapter 2.

Constitutional Basis: Section 34 of the South African Constitution guarantees everyone the right to have disputes resolved by application of law in a fair public hearing before a court or, where appropriate, another independent and impartial tribunal or forum.

Tribunal Structure

Three-Tier System

  • Community Tribunals: Local dispute resolution at community level
  • Regional Councils: Coordination and oversight of community tribunals
  • National Assembly: Policy development and constitutional alignment

Jurisdiction

Community tribunals handle disputes within their geographical area, including:

  • Civil disputes between individuals
  • Contract disagreements
  • Property boundary disputes
  • Community governance issues
  • Constitutional rights violations (advisory capacity)

Procedural Framework

Core Principles

  • Natural Justice: Both parties must be heard
  • Transparency: Proceedings are open unless privacy required
  • Accessibility: No cost barriers to justice
  • Timeliness: Resolution within reasonable timeframes
  • Enforceability: Agreements binding on participants

Integration with Formal Courts

The Tribunal operates as a complementary system, not a replacement for formal courts. Decisions can be formalized through agreement and, where necessary, submitted to traditional courts for enforcement.

Sovereignty Framework

The Tribunal recognizes the inherent sovereignty of individuals while operating within the constitutional framework of South Africa. This balance between personal sovereignty and collective law forms the foundation of our approach.
